How to read a developer's track record before you reserve

20 October 2026 · 6 min read · Updated 20 October 2026

Judge a developer by what it has already handed over, not by what it has launched. Visit a completed community, read the registered project details, and speak to residents before you reserve.

Start with delivered communities, not the launch event

Ask for a list of completed projects and visit one on a weekday evening. Notice how the landscaping has matured, whether lifts and car parks are well kept, and how quickly shops and schools opened. A developer's best marketing is a finished neighbourhood that people still want to live in.

Compare promised and actual handover

Find the original launch announcement for a past project and compare its stated completion window with the real handover. A small slip is normal on large projects; a pattern of long delays is information. Ask the sales team directly how their last three projects performed against schedule.

Check the registration and the paper trail

In Dubai, off-plan projects should be registered with the Dubai Land Department and carry a project registration number. Ask for it, and confirm on the official channels that your unit and the developer match. If something cannot be checked, treat that as an answer.

Talk to owners, not only agents

Residents of a past project can tell you things a brochure cannot: how maintenance responds, how service charges have moved, and whether the facilities match the renders. Owner groups and building managers are usually happy to give an honest view.

Match the developer to your goal

A master-planner with large communities suits buyers who want infrastructure and long-term neighbourhood growth. A design-led boutique developer suits buyers who value architecture and are comfortable with a smaller, riskier track record. Neither is better; they answer different questions.
